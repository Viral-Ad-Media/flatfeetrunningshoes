Many runners head to the gym in their running shoes. For a treadmill session that's fine. For lifting, jumping and side-to-side drills, it isn't ideal, especially if you have flat feet.

![Cross-training](/uploads/2022/05/CROSS-T1.jpg)

## Why running shoes don't suit the gym

Running shoes are built to move you forward. They usually have:

- **Soft, tall cushioning**, which feels unstable under heavy weights
- **A raised heel**, which changes your position in squats and lunges
- **Narrow bases and little lateral support**, which can let your foot roll during side-to-side moves

Flat feet already have less natural stability, so these issues hit harder.

## What flat feet need from a cross-trainer

- **A flat, firm, stable platform** for lifting
- **A wide base and forefoot** so your foot can spread
- **Lateral support** to stop your foot sliding over the edge during cuts and shuffles
- **Midfoot support** or room for an insole
- **Enough flexibility** at the forefoot for jumps and burpees

## Tips for flat-footed gym-goers

1. **Use insoles if you need them.** Many trainers have removable insoles, so you can add your [orthotics](/orthotic-insoles-for-flat-feet-2026/).
2. **Strengthen your feet.** Short-foot exercises, towel scrunches and barefoot balance work build arch strength.
3. **Watch your knees.** In squats and lunges, keep your knees tracking over your toes rather than collapsing inward.
4. **Use the right shoe for the session.** Run in running shoes, lift and train in trainers.
5. **Replace worn shoes.** A compressed or tilted sole reduces stability.

## The bottom line

For flat feet, a good cross-training shoe is flat, firm, wide and supportive at the sides. Keep your running shoes for running, choose a trainer for the gym, and strengthen your feet so they can support you better in both.
