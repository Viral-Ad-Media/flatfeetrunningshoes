If you have flat feet and your ankles roll inward when you run, a stability shoe is usually the best place to start. This guide explains what "stability" actually means in 2026, which features are worth paying for, and how to tell if a shoe is right for you.

## What makes a shoe a stability shoe

Older stability shoes relied on a hard block of dense foam under the arch, called a medial post. Most brands have moved to gentler designs that guide the foot instead of blocking it:

- **Guide rails or sidewalls.** Raised foam along the sides of the heel and midfoot keeps your foot centered without a hard post. The Brooks Adrenaline GTS line popularized this approach.
- **Wider base.** A broader platform under the heel and forefoot is naturally harder to tip over.
- **Firmer medial foam.** Some shoes still use slightly denser foam on the inner side to slow the inward roll.
- **Supportive heel counter.** A structured heel cup holds the rearfoot in place.

## Features that matter most for flat feet

**Midfoot support.** Flat feet need help where the arch would normally be. Press on the inside of the midsole under the arch: it shouldn't fold easily.

**Torsional rigidity.** Hold the heel and toe and twist. A good stability shoe resists twisting through the middle but still flexes at the ball of the foot.

**A roomy but secure fit.** Flat feet often spread when loaded. Look for a toe box with space to splay and a heel that doesn't slip.

**Removable insole.** If you use custom orthotics or [aftermarket insoles](/orthotic-insoles-for-flat-feet-2026/), you'll want to swap the stock one out.

## Shoe lines worth trying

Rather than chase a specific model year, look at the long-running stability lines that brands update every year or so:

- **Brooks Adrenaline GTS** for a balanced everyday trainer with guide rails.
- **ASICS GT-2000 and Gel-Kayano** for a range from lighter daily support to maximum cushioning and structure.
- **Saucony Guide and Hurricane** for soft-riding support.
- **Hoka Arahi and Gaviota** for wide, rockered platforms with lots of cushioning.
- **New Balance 860** for a traditional, dependable stability feel.

Fit varies between brands, so try two or three before deciding.

## How to test a pair

1. **Shop later in the day** when your feet are at their largest.
2. **Bring your running socks** and any insoles you use.
3. **Leave a thumb's width** between your longest toe and the end of the shoe.
4. **Jog for a few minutes** on a treadmill or in the store. Watch for arch pressure, heel slip and hot spots.
5. **Trust comfort.** Research suggests that choosing the shoe that feels most comfortable is one of the best ways to reduce injury risk.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying too much correction.** If you don't overpronate much, a very rigid shoe can feel clunky and shift stress elsewhere.
- **Going too small.** Running shoes usually need to be a half size larger than your everyday shoes.
- **Running shoes into the ground.** Support fades as foam breaks down. See [how often to replace running shoes](/how-often-should-you-replace-running/).

## The bottom line

The right stability shoe for flat feet supports your midfoot, feels secure in the heel, gives your toes room, and feels comfortable from the first few strides. Start with the stability lines above, try them on properly, and let comfort make the final call.
