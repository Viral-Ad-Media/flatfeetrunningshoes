Overpronation is one of the most common reasons flat-footed runners end up with sore shins, knees or arches. The right shoe won't fix everything, but it can take a lot of strain off your legs.

![Overpronation compared to a neutral foot](/uploads/2022/06/OVERPRONATION-1.jpg)

## What causes overpronation

Overpronation happens when the foot rolls inward too far after landing. Common causes include:

- **Low or flat arches**, which give the foot less natural structure
- **Weak hip and glute muscles**, which let the knee drift inward
- **Tight calves and Achilles tendons**, which limit ankle movement and push the foot to compensate
- **Fatigue**, as tired muscles control the foot less well late in a run
- **Body weight**, which increases the load on the arch
- **Worn-out shoes** that no longer provide support

## Signs you might overpronate

- Your shoes wear heavily on the inner edge of the forefoot
- Your heels lean inward when you look at your shoes from behind
- You get pain along the inside of the shin, the arch or the inside of the knee
- On video, your ankles collapse inward after each step

![Overpronation wear and alignment](/uploads/2022/06/OVERPR.jpg)

## What to look for in a shoe

- **Guide rails or a firmer medial side** to slow the inward roll
- **A wide, stable base**, especially through the midfoot
- **A structured heel counter** to keep the rearfoot aligned
- **Enough cushioning** to stay comfortable without feeling mushy and unstable

## Shoe lines to consider

Some of the most popular options for overpronation are **stability models**, such as the Brooks Adrenaline GTS and Glycerin GTS, ASICS GT-2000 and Gel-Kayano, Saucony Guide, Hoka Arahi and New Balance 860.

Some runners with mild overpronation do well in **stable neutral shoes** with a broad base, such as the New Balance 880, Brooks Ghost, Hoka Clifton, Nike Pegasus or ASICS Gel-Nimbus. If you don't have pain, a neutral shoe you find comfortable may be all you need.

## Beyond shoes

Shoes are only part of the answer. These habits help too:

1. **Strengthen your hips and feet.** Clamshells, side planks, single-leg balance and towel scrunches all help you control your stride.
2. **Stretch your calves** daily, especially after runs.
3. **Increase mileage gradually.** Sudden jumps in training load cause more injuries than foot shape does.
4. **Consider insoles.** [Arch support insoles](/arch-support-for-flat-feet-what/) can add support to a shoe you already like.

## When to see a professional

If pain lasts more than a couple of weeks, gets worse as you run, or affects your daily life, see a podiatrist or physiotherapist. A gait analysis can show whether overpronation is really the cause.

## The bottom line

Overpronation is common and manageable. Choose a supportive shoe that feels comfortable, build strength in your hips and feet, and give your body time to adapt.
