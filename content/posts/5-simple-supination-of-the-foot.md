Most foot advice for runners focuses on overpronation. But some feet do the opposite: they roll outward. This is called supination, or underpronation, and it comes with its own set of problems.

![Supination of the foot](/uploads/2022/06/supination-1.jpg)

## 1. What supination is

When you land, your foot naturally rolls slightly inward to absorb shock. With supination, the foot stays on its outer edge or rolls outward instead. Your weight is carried along the outside of the foot and you push off from the smaller toes rather than the big toe.

Supination is more common in people with **high, rigid arches**. It's less common in people with flat feet, but it can still happen, for example after an ankle injury.

## 2. How to spot it

- Your shoes wear mostly on the **outer edge** of the sole
- Your shoes tilt **outward** when placed on a flat surface
- You get frequent **ankle sprains** or feel unstable on uneven ground
- You have pain along the **outside of the foot**, ankle or knee
- Calluses form on the outer edge of the foot or under the little toe

![Supinated foot alignment](/uploads/2022/06/supination-2.jpg)

## 3. What causes it

- **High, rigid arches**, often inherited
- **Previous ankle sprains** that left the ankle weak or stiff
- **Tight calves and Achilles tendons**
- **Weak foot and lower-leg muscles**
- **Worn-out shoes** that have broken down on the outer edge

## 4. Problems it can cause

Because a supinated foot absorbs less shock, more force travels up the leg. That can contribute to ankle sprains, Achilles tendon pain, iliotibial (IT) band pain, plantar fasciitis and stress fractures in the outer foot bones.

## 5. Solutions that help

**Choose cushioned neutral shoes.** Supinators generally do best in well-cushioned neutral shoes with a flexible forefoot. Avoid stability shoes designed to stop inward rolling, as they can make supination worse. Popular cushioned neutral lines include the Brooks Glycerin, Mizuno Wave Rider and Adidas Ultraboost.

**Add cushioned insoles.** A soft insole with a moderate arch and a good heel cup can help spread pressure and absorb shock.

**Stretch and strengthen.** Try daily calf stretches, ankle circles, single-leg balance, resistance-band eversion exercises (pushing the foot outward against a band) and toe raises.

**Replace shoes on time.** Worn outer edges make supination worse. See [how often to replace running shoes](/how-often-should-you-replace-running/).

**Get assessed.** A gait analysis or a podiatrist visit can confirm whether you supinate and whether custom orthotics would help.

## The bottom line

Supination is the opposite of overpronation and needs the opposite approach: more cushioning and flexibility, not more correction. Pick a cushioned neutral shoe, strengthen your ankles and see a professional if pain or sprains keep coming back.
