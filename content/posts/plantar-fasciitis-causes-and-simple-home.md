If your first steps out of bed feel like stepping on a nail under your heel, plantar fasciitis is a likely cause. It's one of the most common causes of heel pain, and runners with flat feet are more prone to it. Most cases improve with simple home care.

![Plantar fasciitis](/uploads/2022/06/FACII-2.jpg)

## What plantar fasciitis is

The plantar fascia is a thick band of tissue running from your heel bone to your toes. It supports your arch and acts like a spring when you walk and run. When it's overloaded, it becomes irritated and painful, usually where it attaches to the heel.

## Common symptoms

- Sharp or stabbing pain at the bottom of the heel
- Pain that's **worst with the first steps in the morning** or after sitting
- Pain that eases with gentle movement but returns after long periods on your feet
- Tenderness when you press on the inside of the heel

## Causes and risk factors

- **Flat feet or very high arches**, which change how load passes through the fascia
- **Tight calves and Achilles tendons**
- **Sudden increases** in running, walking or standing
- **Unsupportive or worn-out shoes**
- **Long hours standing on hard floors**
- **Weight gain**

## Simple home remedies

**1. Stretch your calves and arch.** Do wall calf stretches, and pull your toes back toward your shin before getting out of bed. Hold each stretch for 30 seconds, several times a day.

**2. Roll your arch.** Roll the sole of your foot over a ball or a frozen water bottle for a few minutes. This eases tightness and cold helps with soreness.

**3. Ice after activity.** Apply ice wrapped in a cloth for 10 to 15 minutes.

**4. Wear supportive shoes all day.** Avoid going barefoot on hard floors and flat sandals. Choose shoes with arch support and a cushioned heel.

**5. Add arch support.** An [orthotic insole](/orthotic-insoles-for-flat-feet-2026/) with a deep heel cup can take load off the fascia.

**6. Reduce aggravating activity.** Cut back on running, especially hills and speedwork, and swap in cycling or swimming while it settles.

**7. Strengthen your feet.** Towel scrunches, marble pickups and slow calf raises build support for the arch.

Some people find tools like calf stretchers and foot massagers helpful for regular stretching and massage.

## How long recovery takes

Plantar fasciitis often takes weeks to months to settle. Be consistent with stretching and support even after the pain starts to fade.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor or podiatrist if:

- Pain lasts more than a few weeks despite home care
- You have numbness, tingling or swelling
- The pain started after an injury
- You can't put weight on your foot

They may recommend physical therapy, night splints, custom orthotics or other treatments.

## The bottom line

Plantar fasciitis is common, painful and usually treatable at home. Stretch daily, support your feet, cut back on aggravating activity, and give it time. If it doesn't improve, get it checked.
