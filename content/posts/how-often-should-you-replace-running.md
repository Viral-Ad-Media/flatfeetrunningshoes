Running shoes don't last forever. As the foam breaks down and the structure softens, they give you less cushioning and less support. For flat-footed runners who rely on that support, replacing shoes on time matters even more.

## The rule of thumb

Most running shoes last roughly **300 to 500 miles (about 500 to 800 km)**. Where you fall in that range depends on:

- **Your weight.** Heavier runners compress foam faster.
- **Your stride.** Heavy heel strikers and overpronators wear shoes unevenly.
- **The shoe.** Lightweight racing shoes often wear out sooner than daily trainers.
- **Surfaces.** Rough roads wear outsoles faster than trails or treadmills.

If you run 20 miles a week, that's roughly every four to six months.

## Signs your shoes are worn out

1. **New aches.** Sore shins, knees, arches or heels that weren't there before are often the first sign.
2. **Flat or creased midsoles.** Deep wrinkles in the foam along the sides mean it's compressed.
3. **Worn outsoles.** Smooth patches or exposed midsole foam on the bottom.
4. **Tilting.** Put your shoes on a table and look from behind. If they lean inward or outward, the support has broken down.
5. **They feel "dead."** Less bounce and more ground felt underfoot.
6. **Twisting easily.** A shoe that used to resist twisting now folds easily.

## Why it matters more with flat feet

Stability shoes rely on firmer foam and structure to control overpronation. Once that breaks down, the shoe acts more like a soft neutral shoe, and your foot rolls in more. That's often when [shin splints](/shin-splints-5-things-to-know/) and [plantar fasciitis](/plantar-fasciitis-causes-and-simple-home/) start.

## How to make shoes last longer

- **Use them only for running**, not for errands or the gym
- **Rotate two pairs** so the foam can recover between runs
- **Loosen the laces** to take shoes off rather than kicking them off
- **Let them air-dry** away from radiators and direct heat
- **Track your mileage** with a running app or a note on your phone

## Don't forget your insoles

If you use [orthotic insoles](/orthotic-insoles-for-flat-feet-2026/), check them too. Most OTC insoles need replacing every 6 to 12 months, or when the support feels flattened.

## The bottom line

Plan to replace your running shoes every 300 to 500 miles, and sooner if you notice new aches or visible wear. For flat feet, fresh support is one of the simplest ways to stay injury-free.
