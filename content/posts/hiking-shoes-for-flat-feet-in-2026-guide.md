Trails are harder on flat feet than roads. Rocks, roots and slopes push the foot in every direction, and without a strong arch to brace against, your ankles and arches do extra work. A good hiking shoe gives you the stable platform your feet are missing.

## What flat feet need on the trail

- **A stable, wide base** that resists rolling on uneven ground
- **A firm midsole** that doesn't twist easily through the middle
- **A supportive heel counter** to lock the rearfoot in place
- **Room in the toe box** for downhill sections and foot swelling
- **Good traction** so you aren't bracing your feet on every step

## Shoes, mid boots or full boots?

- **Hiking shoes** are lighter and great for well-kept trails and day hikes.
- **Mid-cut boots** add ankle support, which many flat-footed hikers appreciate on rocky ground or with a pack.
- **Full boots** suit heavy loads and multi-day trips but take longer to break in.

If your ankles tend to roll, a mid boot is a good place to start.

## Fitting tips

1. **Try boots on in the afternoon** with the socks you'll hike in.
2. **Walk down a slope** if the store has one. Your toes shouldn't hit the front.
3. **Check for heel lift.** A little is normal in stiff boots, but it shouldn't rub.
4. **Swap in your insoles.** Many hikers with flat feet replace the stock footbed with a supportive insole, such as [Superfeet Green](/superfeet-green-insoles-review-for-flat/).
5. **Break them in gradually** with short walks before a big hike.

## The bottom line

For flat feet, the best hiking shoe is stable, firm through the middle, roomy in the toes and comfortable from the start. Try a mid boot if your ankles roll, and don't be afraid to add your own insoles.
