Flat feet and wide feet often go together. When the arch drops, the foot lengthens and spreads, so a shoe that fit when you were standing can feel tight a few miles into a run. The good news is that more shoes now come in proper wide sizes, and you don't have to choose between support and comfort.

## Why width matters as much as support

A shoe that's too narrow causes problems even if its arch support is perfect:

- **Your foot hangs over the midsole**, which makes the shoe less stable, not more.
- **Toes get cramped**, leading to blisters, black toenails and irritated bunions.
- **Nerves get squeezed** across the forefoot, which can cause numbness or burning.

## Understanding width sizes

Most running brands offer several widths:

| Men's | Women's | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D | B | Standard |
| 2E | D | Wide |
| 4E | 2E | Extra wide |

Not every model comes in every width, so check before you fall for a shoe. A wide size is built on a different last, not just stretched, so it should fit better than sizing up in length.

## What to look for

- **A broad, rounded toe box** that lets your toes spread when you push off.
- **A wide, stable platform** under the midfoot, so your foot sits on the shoe instead of over the edge.
- **Stretchy, engineered mesh uppers** that give without losing hold.
- **Midfoot support** from guide rails or a firmer medial side if you overpronate.
- **A secure heel**, since wider shoes can sometimes slip at the back.

## Shoe lines that come in wide sizes

Many popular stability and support lines are made in wide and extra-wide versions, including the **Brooks Adrenaline GTS**, **ASICS GT-2000 and Gel-Kayano**, **New Balance 860**, **Saucony Guide** and **Hoka Arahi**. New Balance in particular is known for offering a wide range of widths. Availability changes each season, so check the brand's size chart.

## Fitting tips for wide, flat feet

1. **Measure both feet** standing up. Fit the larger one.
2. **Try the wide version first** instead of going up a half size in a standard width.
3. **Use lacing to fine-tune.** Skip an eyelet over a pressure point, or use a "window" lace to relieve the top of the foot.
4. **Remove the stock insole** and stand on it. If your foot spills over the edges, the shoe is too narrow.
5. **Leave room for insoles.** If you use [orthotic insoles](/orthotic-insoles-for-flat-feet-2026/), bring them to the fitting.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Sizing up instead of widening.** A longer shoe is still narrow, and the extra length makes the flex point sit in the wrong place.
- **Picking soft over stable.** Very soft, narrow-based shoes can feel great in the store and wobbly at mile five.
- **Ignoring discomfort.** A shoe that pinches won't "break in" much. Modern running shoes should feel right from day one.

## The bottom line

For flat, wide feet, look for a shoe that's genuinely wide, not just long, with a stable platform and midfoot support. Try the wide version of a good stability shoe first and use lacing to fine-tune the fit.
