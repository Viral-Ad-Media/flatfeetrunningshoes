Bunions are common in runners with flat feet. First, an honest note: **no exercise or remedy can shrink the bone change behind a bunion**. Only surgery can correct it. But natural, non-surgical steps can ease pain, reduce swelling and may help slow progression.

![Stages of a bunion](/uploads/2022/05/BUNION-1.jpg)

## What a bunion is

A bunion (hallux valgus) is a bony bump at the base of the big toe. It forms as the big toe drifts toward the second toe and the joint is pushed outward. Genetics, foot shape and footwear all play a part. Flat feet and overpronation can add stress to the big-toe joint.

## 1. Wear shoes with a wide toe box

This is the most important step. Shoes that squeeze the forefoot press on the bunion and push the toe further inward. Choose shoes with a **wide, rounded toe box** and soft, stretchy uppers. Many running shoes come in [wide sizes](/running-shoes-for-flat-feet-and-wide-feet-2026-guide/).

![Running shoes for bunions](/uploads/2022/05/BUNION-SHOESX.jpg)

## 2. Support your arch

Supporting a flat arch can reduce the inward roll that loads the big-toe joint. A good [orthotic insole](/orthotic-insoles-for-flat-feet-2026/) may ease pressure on the bunion.

## 3. Use toe spacers and bunion pads

Silicone toe spacers can ease discomfort by gently separating the toes. Gel bunion pads reduce rubbing inside the shoe. They don't change the bone, but they can make shoes more comfortable.

## 4. Do toe and foot exercises

Exercises keep the joint mobile and strengthen the small foot muscles:

- **Toe spreads.** Spread your toes as wide as you can, hold for 5 seconds and repeat.
- **Big-toe pulls.** Gently pull the big toe into a straighter line and hold.
- **Towel scrunches and marble pickups.**
- **Ball rolls** under the sole of the foot.

## 5. Ice and rest when it flares

After a long run or a day on your feet, ice the joint for 10 to 15 minutes to calm swelling. Cut back on activities that make it throb.

## 6. Manage your training load

Sudden increases in mileage, lots of downhill running and speedwork on your toes can all aggravate bunions. Build up gradually and mix in low-impact cross-training.

## 7. Keep a healthy weight

Less load on the forefoot means less pressure on the joint with every step.

## When to see a specialist

See a podiatrist or orthopedic doctor if:

- Pain limits your daily activities or running
- The big toe is crossing over or under the second toe
- The joint is stiff, red or swollen for long periods
- Shoes become hard to wear

They can discuss custom orthotics, injections and, in some cases, surgery.

## The bottom line

You can't shrink a bunion naturally, but you can make it much more comfortable. Wear roomy shoes, support your arch, protect the joint and keep your feet strong.
