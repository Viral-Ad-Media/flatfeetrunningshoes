Orthotic insoles are one of the most common recommendations for flat feet, and for good reason. The right pair can ease arch fatigue, reduce strain on your shins and knees, and make your favorite shoes feel more supportive. The wrong pair can just move the pain somewhere else.

![Orthotic insoles](/uploads/2022/05/insoles-3.jpg)

## How orthotics help flat feet

Orthotic insoles support the arch, cradle the heel and help control how far the foot rolls inward. For flat feet that can mean:

- Less strain on the plantar fascia and arch muscles
- Better alignment from the ankle up to the knee
- More even pressure across the sole
- Less fatigue on long runs or long days standing

## Over-the-counter vs custom

**Over-the-counter (OTC) orthotics** are mass-produced insoles in standard shapes and sizes. They're affordable, easy to try and work well for many people with flexible flat feet.

**Custom orthotics** are made from a mold or scan of your feet, usually prescribed by a podiatrist. They cost more but suit people with rigid flat feet, significant pain, uneven feet or conditions such as diabetes.

A sensible approach for most runners is to try a good OTC insole first. If it doesn't help within a few weeks, see a podiatrist.

## Choosing an OTC orthotic

1. **Firmness.** Semi-rigid insoles, with a firm shell and cushioned top, are the best starting point for flat feet.
2. **Arch height.** Choose a low-to-medium arch if your feet are flat. Too high a profile can create a painful pressure point.
3. **Heel cup.** A deep heel cup adds stability and helps with heel pain.
4. **Length.** Full-length insoles replace the stock insole entirely. 3/4-length insoles fit into tighter shoes.
5. **Volume.** Match the thickness to your shoe. High-volume insoles need roomy running shoes or boots.

## Breaking in orthotics

- Day 1: wear them for an hour or two
- Add an hour or so each day
- Walk in them for several days before running
- Expect some arch tiredness at first, but not sharp pain, numbness or blisters

## When orthotics aren't enough

Orthotics work best alongside good shoes, sensible training and strength work. If you still have pain, consider a [stability running shoe](/running-shoes-for-flat-feet-stability-2026-guide/), foot and hip strengthening, and a visit to a podiatrist or physiotherapist.

## The bottom line

Start with a semi-rigid, low-to-medium arch orthotic that fits your shoes, break it in gradually, and give it a few weeks. If your pain doesn't improve, custom orthotics prescribed by a professional are the next step.
